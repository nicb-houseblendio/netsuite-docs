You can use a passkey instead of your password during the login process to your NetSuite account. Passkeys are available for all roles. Passkeys can be either physical or digital, for example a face ID or fingerprint.

Important:

You must still remember your NetSuite password, because passkeys are only available for the login process. You still need a password for other processes, for example 2FA reset.

You can manage your passkeys using the **Manage Passkeys** link in the **Settings** portlet when you're logged in to NetSuite.

#### To manage your passkeys:

1.  Find the **Settings** portlet on the Home page. If you can't locate the **Settings** portlet, see [Finding Your Settings Portlet](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/bridgehead_1493644302.html).
    
    Tip:
    
    The **Settings** portlet can appear anywhere on a customized Home page (or dashboard). By default, it's located on the left side, and you might need to scroll down to see it.
    
2.  Click **Manage Passkeys**.
    
3.  On the **Passkeys** page, you can see the list of your passkeys. On this page you can click the **Create New** button to create a passkey, you can edit your existing passkeys, or delete them.
    
    Important:
    
    When creating a new passkey, always use a FIDO-certified option. Passkeys that are not certified can provide a risk for your NetSuite account, because they may not be resistant to potential attackers.
    
    Consider choosing passkeys that comply with FIDO2 specification.
    

As of NetSuite 2026.2, you can use passkeys instead of an authenticator apps for two-factor authentication (2FA) in the following cases:

-   The passkey you use is FIDO2 compliant.
    
-   Your Administrator doesn't disable the Passkey as 2FA feature, reverting 2FA settings to their previous state.
    
-   You need to generate the 2FA code to log in to NetSuite.
    

Important:

If you use a passkey for 2FA, NetSuite asks you for a code form your authenticator app every three months. This is to ensure that your 2FA setup is working properly and can be used to authenticate during a 2FA reset.
