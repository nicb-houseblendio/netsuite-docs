SuiteScript 2.1 includes the following global objects. You can use these objects in your scripts without loading them as dependencies.

-   [define Object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387811519.html)
    
-   [require Function](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387813188.html)
    
-   [log Object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387812838.html)
    
-   [util Object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387813290.html)
    
-   [toString()](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4542785226.html)
    
-   [JSON object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387812540.html)
    
-   [Promise Object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387812940.html)
    
-   [Iterator](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_0831085754.html)
    

Note:

In JavaScript, all functions are objects. The [define Object](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387811519.html) and [require Function](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4387813188.html) topics discuss `define()` and `require()` used by SuiteScript 2.1 to load and define modules.

Note:

Also, to safely use the require object or the N/log and N/util modules, you should specifically import those modules as dependencies of your scripts, especially in your SuiteScript 2.1 scripts.
