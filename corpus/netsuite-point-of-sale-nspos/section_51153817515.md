## Cash Drawers {#subsect_32174852094}

The physical portion of a till is also called a cash drawer. It is the drawer or cabinet in which cash payments are placed and cash due back to the customer (also called "change") is removed.

Note:

The person responsible for a till is often referred to as the cashier, the sales associate, user, or operator. Because we are discussing "cash drawers," here we'll use the term **cashier**.

During a shift or business day, the cashier will use the till to take cash payments for sales, provide refunds for returns, take deposits for sales orders, and perform other transactions as needed.

If a cashier runs short of the cash needed to make change, they can get funds added through a drawer loan. If they have more cash in their till than permitted by store guidelines, they can make a drawer drop.

An example setup is where the cashier has one portable till that they keep with them throughout their shift. They maintain possession of the till until control is passed to store management for counting. The till is then moved to a secure location, such as a safe or vault, or passed to the next cashier to begin their shift. This setup requires assistance from NetSuite Professional Services.

Most till management tasks performed by a manager are manual, such as using NSPOS to enter the beginning and ending cash amount. These tasks are important for recording deposit amounts and determining any overages or shortages.

## Tills/Cash Drawers in NSPOS {#subsect_71155028892}

NSPOS tills can work in these modes:

-   **Register (Till) Accountability** mode (default)
    
-   **Operator (Till) Accountability** mode
    
-   Generic mode in which the NSPOS till is not mapped to a physical cash drawer
    

To understand these modes, we need to define the difference between Till Sessions, User Sessions, and a Business Day.

-   [Till Session](#subsect_22142818402)
    
-   [User Session](#subsect_46143455269)
    
-   [Business Day](#subsect_81142937672)
    

### Till Session {#subsect_22142818402}

A till session is the period a till was active during a shift or business day.

| Till Session |
| --- |
| **Assigned to:**
-   Cashier
-   Till

 |
| **Starts:** When cashier logs into NSPOS **Ends:** By running End of Day (EOD) If your business uses Operator (Till) Accountability mode, the till session ends when the cashier logs out of NSPOS selects to **Take** the till. Logging in again starts a new till session. If the cashier chooses **Lock** when logging out, the session does not end. |
| **Usage notes:**

-   Till sessions don't have to start and end within a single calendar date. For example, an overnight session can cross over to the next calendar date.
-   For EOD, till session records are created in NetSuite ERP with any overages or shortages. These records include the session's **Till ID** for reference, and use the accounts configured in your RA-Money Management Action setups. See [Tracking Till Money Movement through NetSuite ERP records](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_1513280696.html).
-   When running a report, you can use the **Till ID** as search criteria to filter transactions. For example, see the [Sales Audit Report](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4533010281.html).

 |

### User Session {#subsect_46143455269}

A user session is the period a cashier is logged into NSPOS.

| User Session |
| --- |
| **Assigned to:** Specific cashier |
| **Starts:** When cashier logs into NSPOS **Ends:** When cashier manually logs out, is automatically logged out, or during EOD. |
| **Usage notes:**
-   **Business Day** - The business day logged to NSPOS transactions uses the calendar date on which the user session began.
-   **Idle Time-outs** - A register will automatically switch to **Closed Mode** after a set amount of time has passed without any input from the cashier. See [Idle Time-outs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_4539107183.html#bridgehead_1540840949) in the employee section of this guide.

 |

Important:

**About transaction dates and overnight shifts** - A user session begins when a cashier logs in and ends when that cashier logs out. All transactions made by the cashier are assigned to the calendar date on which their session started. That date is the "business day."

Some retailers are open past midnight, with shifts that start in the evening and end on the next calendar date. For example, the cashier logs in at 10:00 PM on June 2nd and logs off at 6:00 AM on June 3rd.

Because the session business day started on June 2nd, all transactions made after midnight, on June 3rd, will show as having occurred on the June 2nd business day.

For reports, you can set filters to see the "true" transaction calendar dates. See [Filtering a Sales Audit Report](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4533010281.html#subsect_26141002581) for an example.

Tip:

Running **End of Day** closes all open sessions.

### Business Day {#subsect_81142937672}

A business day typically starts when a store opens and ends when the store closes. Stores that are open 24 hours use their own definition of a business day. A business day can include one or more user sessions, till sessions, or both. It can also cross two calendar days.

Running End of Day closes all open sessions for a business day.

Note:

A calendar day begins at 12:00 AM and ends at 11:59 PM. It comprises the calendar "date" only.

| Business Day |
| --- |
| **Starts:** When cashier or manager logs into NSPOS **Ends:** When End of Day (EOD) is performed |
| **Usage notes:**
-   Transactions occurring on a business day are linked to till sessions logged on that day.
-   Currency held in the cash drawer and exchanged for sales, refunds, and so on, are linked to a day's till session
-   When the store closes, perform EOD to close the workday and complete other procedures used by your business, such as running **Count and Settle Till**.
-   You can configure the EOD process with different options based on your needs.

 |

Tip:

Before starting any cash sales activity on a NSPOS terminal, you should perform **Start of the Day** and count and enter the starting currency values of the till (for each operator's shift). Accurate reporting depends on accurate starting values and accurate sales transactions.

Next, we describe the NSPOS till accountability options and how they relate to till sessions, user sessions, and business days.

### Register (Till) Accountability vs. Operator (Till) Accountability {#subsect_80143535410}

These tables describe the till-count modes as they relate to your registers and cashiers. End of Day (EOD) and flag details to choose a mode are included. For the steps to find and enable/disable a flag, see [Flags and Settings](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_4470908436.html).

| Register (Till) Accountability |
| --- |
| **Till Assignment:** Register Multiple cashiers can use the same till on one register. The till is assigned to the register.
-   Any cashier that logs into the register throughout the day can use the till.
-   The first cashier to log in is considered to be responsible for the till.

 |
| **Cashier** The shift starts by entering the total cash amount in the till or the quantities of each cash denomination. Entering the denomination quantities uses the **Count Till** function. **Multiple registers at a time** - Cashier can log into multiple registers at the same time using the same authentication credentials. |
| **End of Day (EOD)** Running EOD closes the register's till session. Although any employee with EOD access can perform the count, the last cashier using the register should perform EOD and close the till session using [Count and Settle Till](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_0609120634.html#subsect_88124656430). You can count and settle outside of the EOD process. |
| **Flag to select:** Till: Operator Accountability

-   Disabled (clear)
-   Default is: Disabled

 |

| Operator (Till) Accountability Mode |
| --- |
| **Till Assignment:** Cashier Till is assigned to the cashier, not the register. The cashier takes their till if they move to a different register. This mobility allows different cashiers to use the same register during a single shift or day. Assignment starts when cashier logs into NSPOS. No other cashiers can use the till until it's released using End of Day (EOD). If a till is needed immediately, but the cashier has locked the till by logging off NSPOS, a manager can run **Remove Till Assignment**. The next cashier to log in will be assigned the till. |
| **Cashier** Begins their shift by entering the total cash amount in the till or the quantities of each cash denomination. Entering the denomination quantities uses the **Count Till** function. Cashier's till can be used on any register the cashier might work on during their shift. When a cashier logs out, they choose whether to keep their till assignment with the register:
-   **Take** - Cashier won't return to the register. Current till session is ended and register is ready for the next cashier-assigned till. If cashier doesn't take their till, no other cashiers can use the register.
-   **Lock** - Cashier will return to the register later in the day or shift. No other cashier can log in to the register (unless an unassigned till is available). The current till session remains active and resumes when the cashier logs in again.

**One register at a time** - When using a single set of authentication credentials, cashier is limited to logging into one register at a time. Things to note:

-   Choosing **Lock** when logging out is treated as though the cashier is still logged in on the register. When the till is locked, it can't be used on another register.
-   An idle time-out causing a register to switch to **Closed Mode** is for till purposes the same as choosing **Lock**. The cashier remains logged in with their till restricted to that register only.
-   There can be a delay when replicating a cashier's logged in or out status to other registers. This delay may prevent a cashier from immediately moving from one register to another.

Note: The single-register limitation applies within a location only. Due to replication processes, a cashier could log into one register at one location and log into a different register at another location, without logging out at the first location. |
| **End of Day (EOD)** Running EOD closes the cashier's till session. Cashier must run EOD on their till. EOD can't be performed by another employee. |
| **Flag to select:** Till: Operator Accountability

-   Enabled (checked)
-   Default is: Disabled

Operator Accountability can be configured globally or on a per-workstation basis. |

Tip:

Under **Operator (Till) Accountability** mode, you can have more than one till for an NSPOS register. Configuring NSPOS for this scenario requires assistance from NetSuite Professional Services.
